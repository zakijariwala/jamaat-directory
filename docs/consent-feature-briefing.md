# A proposal for the committee: ask people before we list them

*Please read this and decide: should we build this or not?*

This paper explains one proposed change to the KSIJ Travel Directory. It is
written in plain words. No technical knowledge is needed.

---

## What we do now

Someone fills in the form to add a contact. That entry waits. A committee
moderator reads it and approves it. Only then does it show on the site.

We do not ask the person whose number was added. We trust the person who
submitted it.

## What we are proposing

When someone adds another person's number, the system contacts that person by
itself. It sends them a WhatsApp message and, if we have their email, an email
too. The message says:

> Your number was added to the KSIJ Travel Directory. Did you agree to this?
> **Yes** or **No**.

Then:

- They tap **Yes** → the entry goes live on its own.
- They tap **No** → the entry is blocked and never shows.
- They do **not reply in 3 days** → a committee member decides, the way we do
  today.
- If a person adds **their own** number, we do not message them.

## What changes from today

| | Today | With this change |
|---|---|---|
| Who checks the person agreed | Nobody asks them | The person confirms it themselves |
| Committee work | Read and approve every entry | Only handle the ones with no reply |
| How fast an entry appears | After a moderator gets to it | Right after the person taps Yes |
| Who gets contacted | Nobody | The person whose number was added |

## The good

- **The person agrees for themselves.** That is the strongest kind of consent.
- **Less work for the committee.** Most entries approve themselves.
- **Entries appear faster.** No waiting for a moderator.
- **More trust and privacy.** We only list people who said yes.
- **A clear record.** We can show the person agreed, and when.

## The concerns

- **It can feel like spam.** We message people who did not ask to hear from us.
- **WhatsApp can block our number.** WhatsApp does not like businesses messaging
  people who never opted in. If it blocks us, this whole check stops working.
- **It costs money.** WhatsApp charges a small fee for each message. Email is
  free.
- **It can be misused.** Someone could put in a stranger's number just to make
  the system message them.
- **No person checks a "Yes".** When someone taps Yes, the entry goes live with
  no committee member looking at it.
- **Setup takes time.** WhatsApp requires a verified business account and message
  approvals before we can send anything. That is days of paperwork, not minutes.

## What it costs

- **Email:** basically free.
- **WhatsApp:** a small fee per message, plus a verified WhatsApp business
  account. There is no way to send WhatsApp messages automatically for free.
- **Setup:** one-time effort to register the business account and get messages
  approved.

## What we ask you to decide

**Do we build this, yes or no?**

If yes, two choices are already set, and you can change them:

1. A **Yes** from the person publishes the entry automatically.
2. **No reply in 3 days** holds the entry for a committee member.

If you would rather keep a person checking every entry, or skip WhatsApp and use
email only, say so. Both are possible and both cost less.
